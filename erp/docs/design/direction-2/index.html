Hiqbah ERP · Direction II · 6 Oct 2026

The quiet ledgerسجلّ هادئ

A second direction for the roastery's ERP. One canvas, one sheet, a Naskh voice for titles, light numerals for figures, and visuals that only a roastery would have. Business logic, permissions and data are untouched; every screen below is real HTML at 1440px, in Arabic, right to left, with illustrative data.

01

What was wrong with the first direction

Read against the Figma exploration, the brand mark in the repository and the craft principles you asked for, the first redesign failed in eight ways.

01
It was a document about a product, not a product. The screens were small wireframes inside a 17-section report. Nothing was composed at real size, so nothing could feel premium.
Six full 1440px screens, composed and checked at native size; the explanation is short and sits around them.
02
Box grammar survived. Panes with borders, a boxed metric strip, grey kanban columns, a bordered table frame. Same template DNA as before, only paler.
Two surfaces only: the canvas and one white sheet. Inside the sheet, hairlines and space do the grouping.
03
Typography had no voice. One family at 12–15px for everything; page titles at 20–22px; numbers in the text face. Flat, technical, generic.
A Naskh display face for titles and section heads, light Geist numerals for figures, Plex Arabic for the working UI.
04
The brand was missing. Espresso-green buttons and a generic tag palette ignored the Figma sage-and-sand direction and the logo's Naskh ح with its ember dash.
Sage for action and selection, sand for comparison, ember only for the mark and the live “now” line.
05
Status was noisy. Every status was a tinted tag with a dot, so a 12-row table carried 24 coloured shapes.
Status is a dot and a word. Tint is reserved for the few states that need a person: overdue, blocked, bottleneck.
06
Dashboards were still a grid of panes. Metric strip, a queue in a pane, charts in panes: the shape of every admin template.
A sentence first, one hero figure, then bands on hairlines. Each band answers one question.
07
Generic visuals. Line and bar charts that could belong to any SaaS product.
Roastery-native visuals: the roaster's hour track, green-coffee cover against supplier lead time, loss on the blend's band, gram reconciliation.
08
Density tuned for a spec, not for eight hours. 40px rows, 13px cells, 12px meta everywhere: efficient, but tiring and cramped.
14px text, 48px rows, 48px gutters by default; compact 40px density stays available for ledgers and movements.
02

Direction II in six rules

Kept from the Figma exploration: the light ground, sage and sand, large Arabic titles, unboxed figures, the single priority line. Changed: the shell, the type system, density, status, charts and every page composition.

Two surfaces

Canvas for navigation, one white sheet for work. Overlays (drawer, palette, popover) are the only other layer.

Type carries hierarchy

Markazi Text for page and section titles; IBM Plex Sans Arabic 14px for the UI; Geist for numbers, light at large sizes.

Say it first

Dashboards open with a one-sentence reading. Numbers support the sentence, not the other way round.

Colour is a signal

Neutral by default. Sage = action and selection. Sand = comparison. Ember = brand mark and “now”. Red only for overdue or failure.

Native to a roastery

Batches, lots, grams, cover days and roaster hours get their own visual forms instead of generic widgets.

Calm motion

120–220ms, ease-out (.23,1,.32,1), presses scale to .97, drawers slide from the end side, nothing bounces, all off under reduced motion.

Self-review: what still felt generic, and what changed

Each screen was rendered at 1440px and inspected after the first build. These are the faults found and the changes made in round two. All of them are visible in the screens above.

Exec chart read as decline.
The daily line was jagged (weekend dips) and, with time running right to left, a rising month read as a fall to anyone used to Latin charts. It also ran past the page gutter.
Cumulative month-to-date against the same days of August, so the gap is the story. Quantitative charts now run left to right, as Saudi banking and market apps do; schedules and process tracks stay right to left. Width fitted to the content column.
The roaster track was a toy.
Squeezed into a third of the page, its labels clipped to “خلط”, and the batches were an hour long on a W15, which roasts 15 kg, not 24.
It is now a full-width band and the signature of the operations page: a W30, roughly 30-minute cycles on a 07:00–13:00 track, a hatched maintenance slot, and the live 10:42 line. Day figures were corrected to match three finished batches (61.2 kg).
The orders table hid under its own drawer.
An overlay drawer covered the payment column, and filter chips wrapped onto two lines.
A split view: the list stays fully visible and drops its secondary columns (quantity, city, owner) while the preview is open. A note says the columns come back on close. Chips and search no longer wrap.
Two primary buttons per row.
“تأكيد الشحن” rendered as a filled button in every ready row, and a class clash greyed its label.
Row actions are all secondary. The page header keeps the only primary action.
Brand noise in the chrome.
A floating Latin “ERP” label, a bell with an ember dot that duplicated the inbox count, and an avatar whose “إ” read as “!”.
The wordmark plus an Arabic descriptor; the bell is gone because the inbox is the notification centre; the avatar uses two initials (إع).
Bidi slips.
Week ranges in the bar chart rendered as “29–22”, “1 × 750 غ” reordered, and the PO number aligned to the wrong edge above its title.
Ranges and quantities are LTR-isolated, and IDs are inline isolates rather than block-level LTR.

Still weak, honestly

The sidebar is conventional.
Seventeen items with icons is the Linear/Attio pattern. It is calm, but not distinctive.
Next step: role-scoped navigation, so a roaster sees about 6 items and the owner sees everything, plus pinned views.
The paired cash bars are generic.
They are honest and readable, but any SaaS product could have drawn them.
Candidate: a cash-gap view that shows days of sales not yet collected per week, which ties back to the 11-day lag in the lede.
Only desktop at 1440px.
Tablet (the floor) and phone (reps, approvals) are not drawn in this direction yet.
The production page and the ops roaster band should be drawn next at 1024px with touch density.
Dark mode is only defined.
Tokens exist for dark, but no screen has been reviewed in it.
Light ships first; dark needs its own review pass.
